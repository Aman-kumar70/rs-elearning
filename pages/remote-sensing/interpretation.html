Reading a Satellite Image

Before you ever run an algorithm, a trained analyst can identify most features in a satellite image with the naked eye — using the same seven visual clues photo-interpreters have used since aerial photography began. This is the skill that makes every other chapter useful.

05.1 / The Seven Elements

The detective clues of image interpretation

Interpretation is the act of examining images to identify objects and judge their significance — turning raw pixels into information. It's built on comparing what you see against what you already know, using seven recurring visual elements.

01 / TONE

Tone

The relative brightness or colour of an object. The most fundamental element — without tonal difference, none of the others would be visible.

Example: Water is typically dark in NIR; dry sand is bright across most bands.
02 / SHAPE

Shape

The general form, structure or outline of an individual object — often the first clue to what something is.

Example: A perfectly straight line is almost always man-made (road, canal, runway); a meandering line is usually a natural river.
03 / SIZE

Size

A function of scale — always judge relative to other objects and to the known scale of the image.

Example: A large rectangular building suggests a factory or warehouse; a small one suggests a house.
04 / PATTERN

Pattern

The spatial arrangement of repeated objects — orderly repetition produces a distinctive, recognisable signature.

Example: Evenly spaced orchard trees or regularly spaced houses in a subdivision.
05 / TEXTURE

Texture

The frequency of tonal change within an area — one of the most important elements for distinguishing features, especially in radar imagery.

Example: Forest canopy = coarse, mottled texture; still water = smooth, uniform texture.
06 / SHADOW

Shadow

Reveals the profile and relative height of a target — sometimes the only clue to an object's true 3D shape.

Example: A tower and a flat parking lot marking might look identical from directly above; their shadows are completely different.
07 / ASSOCIATION

Association

The relationship between an object and its surroundings — proximity to other recognisable features that helps confirm identification.

Example: A school is typically associated with playgrounds and sports fields; a factory with rail sidings or loading docks.

05.2 / True Colour vs False Colour

Why satellite images often look "wrong"

A normal colour photograph maps blue-green-red light to blue-green-red on your screen — matching human vision. A false-colour composite instead assigns a non-visible band (usually NIR) to one of the display channels, most commonly displaying NIR as red. This is deliberate: it exploits vegetation's strong NIR reflectance to make vegetation leap out in vivid red, at the cost of no longer looking "natural."

The Physics of Additive RGB Display Synthesis

Satellite sensors do not take "colour photographs" — they measure reflected electromagnetic energy in discrete spectral intervals and record them as grayscale Digital Numbers (DN: 0–255). A computer monitor creates all colours through additive synthesis using three physical display channels: Red, Green, and Blue. Any three satellite bands can be loaded into these three guns. When the bands match human vision (Red→Red, Green→Green, Blue→Blue), you get a True Colour Composite. When non-visible bands (such as Near-Infrared or Shortwave-Infrared) are routed into visible display guns, you produce a False Colour Composite (FCC).

🌿
The Core Question

Why is healthy vegetation bright red in Standard False Colour?
In a Standard False Colour Composite (CIR / Standard FCC), space agencies assign:
• Near-Infrared (NIR) Band → Red Display Gun
• Visible Red Band → Green Display Gun
• Visible Green Band → Blue Display Gun
Healthy plant leaves contain chlorophyll, which absorbs 90–95% of visible red light for photosynthesis. However, the spongy mesophyll cell structure strongly reflects 40–50% of incoming Near-Infrared light to prevent thermal overheating. Routing high-intensity NIR into the monitor's Red gun while almost no light is sent to the Green gun causes vigorous vegetation to blaze in vibrant crimson red. If foliage suffers drought, pest disease, or dies, the mesophyll collapses and NIR reflection plummets, causing the red color to fade to dull pink or brown long before human eyes see visible wilting.

Standard False Colour (NIR · Red · Green) Interpretation Key

The universal remote sensing field guide for interpreting Standard FCC satellite imagery (Landsat Band 4,3,2 / Landsat-8 Band 5,4,3 / Sentinel-2 Band 8,4,3 / IRS LISS-III Band 3,2,1):

Earth Surface Target Standard FCC Display Colour Physical / Spectral Mechanism
Dense Healthy Vegetation (Forests, Vigorous Crops) Bright Red to Deep Crimson Massive NIR scatter by internal spongy mesophyll → High Red gun DN (~200+); strong visible red absorption by chlorophyll → Low Green gun DN (~25).
Sparse / Stressed Vegetation, Grassland & Scrub Pale Pink, Tan to Mottled Red Reduced leaf cell density lowers NIR reflectance; dry soil background reflectance mixes into the pixel.
Clear Deep Water (Lakes, Reservoirs, Open Ocean) Dark Navy Blue to Jet Black Pure water absorbs nearly 100% of NIR and SWIR radiation, and most visible red. Near-zero radiance enters any gun (DN ≈ 0–5).
Turbid / Silt-Laden Water (Rivers, Coastal Plumes) Cyan to Powder Light Blue Suspended mineral silt and clay scatter visible Green and Red light into the Green and Blue display guns, while NIR remains absorbed.
Bare Dry Soil & Fallow Fields Light Brown, Yellow-Grey to White Dry mineral soils exhibit monotonic, steadily rising reflectance from visible to NIR, illuminating all three guns roughly equally.
Moist / Saturated Soil Dark Brown to Dark Greyish-Black Pore water in soil absorbs Near-Infrared energy, drastically lowering overall scene brightness across all channels.
Urban Built-Up Areas, Concrete & Asphalt Cyan, Silvery-Grey to Steel Blue Asphalt and concrete reflect evenly across visible Green and Red channels with low-to-medium NIR, producing a characteristic cyan-grey tone.
Snow & Glacial Ice Brilliant Bright White Extremely high albedo across all visible and NIR bands saturates all three display guns (R=255, G=255, B=255).
Cloud Cover & Fog Brilliant Bright White Liquid water droplets in dense clouds scatter all optical wavelengths equally. Casts distinct black cloud shadows to the west/northwest.
Major Operational Band Combinations in Space Agencies

Beyond Standard False Colour, operational remote sensing workflows use specialized combinations tailored to specific physical phenomena:
• Natural / True Colour (Red, Green, Blue): Sentinel-2 (4,3,2) · Landsat-8/9 (4,3,2) · Landsat-7 (3,2,1). Matches human vision for public communication and coastal water clarity.
• Standard False Colour / CIR (NIR, Red, Green): Sentinel-2 (8,4,3) · Landsat-8/9 (5,4,3) · IRS LISS-III (3,2,1). The gold standard for vegetation biomass, forest cover, and crop health.
• SWIR Agriculture (SWIR-1, NIR, Red): Sentinel-2 (11,8,4) · Landsat-8/9 (6,5,4). Sharpens crop vigor, soil moisture dynamics, and exposes fresh wildfire burn scars in charcoal/red.
• Snow vs. Cloud Discrimination (SWIR-1, NIR, Green): Sentinel-2 (11,8,3) · Landsat-8/9 (6,5,3). Ice absorbs SWIR (turning snow bright cyan/blue), while clouds reflect SWIR (remaining brilliant white), effortlessly resolving snowfields from cloud tops.
• Geology & Mineralogy (SWIR-2, SWIR-1, Red): Sentinel-2 (12,11,4) · Landsat-8/9 (7,6,4). Exposes hydrothermal alteration minerals, quartzite, and fault lines invisible in natural color.

05.3 / Putting It Together — Real Scenes

Reading real satellite images

Here's the payoff: applying all seven elements at once to identify real features in real IRS and SPOT imagery, the way an IIRS-trained analyst would.

Try It Yourself

Next time you open Google Earth or NASA Worldview, pick any unfamiliar feature and work through the seven elements in order: tone, shape, size, pattern, texture, shadow, association. You'll be surprised how often you can identify something — an airport, a mine, a reservoir, a farm — without any external information at all.

Key Takeaways
  • Image interpretation relies on seven visual elements: tone, shape, size, pattern, texture, shadow, association.
  • Tone is foundational — without brightness/colour contrast none of the other elements would be visible at all.
  • False-colour composites (commonly NIR→red) trade "natural" appearance for dramatically better vegetation and water discrimination.
  • Interpretation accuracy depends on the analyst's training, knowledge of the area, and the quality/resolution of the imagery.
  • These same principles — especially texture and tone — are exactly what classification algorithms (IP.4) try to encode mathematically.
