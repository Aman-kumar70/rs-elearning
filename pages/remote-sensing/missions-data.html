How the Family Works Together

Monitoring crop health across India: AWiFS (56m) images the whole country every 5 days — spot problems early. LISS-III (23.5m) gives district-level detail. LISS-IV (5.8m) zooms into specific problem areas. PAN (sub-metre) identifies individual farm boundaries. This multi-resolution strategy is the backbone of India's EO programme design — exactly the resolution trade-off from RS.4, deliberately engineered around.

06.3 / Getting Real Data — Free

Where to download satellite imagery today

Most satellite data is free — no fee, no special access, just registration. This is the step most tutorials skip and where most beginners get stuck.

Free · India

Bhoonidhi (NRSC)

ISRO's official data portal. IRS, Cartosat, Resourcesat, RISAT data. Free for registered users.

bhoonidhi.nrsc.gov.in
Free · USA

USGS EarthExplorer

The largest free archive. Landsat (since 1972), Sentinel-2, MODIS. Search by location, date, cloud cover.

earthexplorer.usgs.gov
Free · Europe

Copernicus Data Space

ESA's portal for Sentinel-1 (radar), Sentinel-2 (optical), Sentinel-3. 5-day global revisit.

dataspace.copernicus.eu
Free · Google

Google Earth Engine

Petabytes of data pre-loaded in the cloud. Write code to process imagery without downloading anything.

earthengine.google.com
Processing Levels — What "L2A" Actually Means

Data ships at different processing stages. L0/L1: raw or minimally-processed telemetry — not geo-referenced, essentially engineering data. L2: converted to a geophysical quantity (surface reflectance), calibrated and geo-referenced — the level you generally want for analysis. L3/L4: mapped onto a uniform grid, quality-controlled, sometimes combined with models (e.g. NDVI composites). For almost any project, look for "L2A" (Sentinel-2) or "Collection 2 Level-2" (Landsat) — atmospherically corrected, ready to use.

💡
Plain English

Think of a supermarket meal kit. L0/L1 is raw ingredients straight from the farm — flour, eggs, raw wheat — technically food, but you can't eat it yet. L2 is a meal kit with pre-measured, washed, calibrated ingredients — ready to cook, comparable across brands. L3/L4 is the fully cooked, plated meal — just serve it. Almost nobody wants raw wheat (L0/L1); almost everybody wants at least the meal kit (L2) or better.

search_and_download.py — programmatic access via STAC
# Most modern archives (Copernicus, USGS, Planetary Computer) expose a
# STAC (SpatioTemporal Asset Catalog) API — searchable without a browser.
from pystac_client import Client

catalog = Client.open("https://earth-search.aws.element84.com/v1")

search = catalog.search(
    collections=["sentinel-2-l2a"],           # atmospherically corrected, analysis-ready
    bbox=[76.7, 30.6, 77.4, 31.2],          # [min_lon, min_lat, max_lon, max_lat] — e.g. Dehradun
    datetime="2025-10-01/2025-12-31",
    query={"eo:cloud_cover": {"lt": 10}}   # < 10% cloud cover only
)

items = list(search.items())
print(f"Found {len(items)} scenes")

# Each item exposes direct links to individual bands (COG format)
best = items[0]
red_url = best.assets["red"].href
nir_url = best.assets["nir"].href
print(red_url)

Key Takeaways
  • Landsat gives the longest continuous record (since 1972); Sentinel-1/2 give free, frequent optical + all-weather radar.
  • India's sensor family (AWiFS → LISS-III → LISS-IV → PAN) spans 56m down to 0.25m by design, not accident.
  • Satellite data is free from Bhoonidhi, USGS, and Copernicus — registration only.
  • Look for Level-2A / Level-2 products — atmospherically corrected and ready to analyze.
  • Modern archives are programmatically searchable via STAC APIs — no browser required, and this is exactly how you'll fetch data in the Python Lab (IP.5).
